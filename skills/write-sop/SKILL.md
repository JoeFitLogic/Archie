# write-sop

The input is the owner describing how something is done — often rough or dictated. Turn it into an SOP that someone who has never done the task could follow first time.

## Structure

**SOP: [task name]**

**Purpose:** one line on why this matters.

**When to use it:** the trigger — what happens that means this SOP starts.

**Who does it:** the role (e.g. VA, owner).

**What you need:** logins, tools, templates and links. Use `[CHECK: link]` for anything referred to but not given.

**Steps**
Numbered. One action per step, starting with a verb. Where it helps, add "✓ Looks like:" with what the result should look like, so they can check their own work.

**Watch out for:** the 2–4 most likely mistakes, and how to avoid each.

**Done when:** the exact finished state, and who to tell.

## Rules
- Plain UK English. Short sentences. No jargon without a one-line explanation.
- Keep the owner's order of steps unless it clearly wouldn't work, then note it as `[CHECK: order]`.
- Never guess at logins, prices or client details — mark gaps with `[CHECK: …]`.
- Output only the SOP, ready to paste into a doc.
